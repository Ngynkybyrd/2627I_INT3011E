\section{Planned expanded experimental methods}
\label{sec:expanded-methods-en}
\textbf{Status: a prospective design under the accepted contract/schema v2;
this is not an expanded-results section.}
The target is 16,000 puzzles and, under the accepted matrix, 330,000 main runs.
These are target denominators, not counts of completed puzzles or runs.
The original S80/B16/C10 experiment with 906 runs remains separate;
pilot, fixture, and expanded observations will not be pooled into its statistics.
This framework is not included in either main report.

\subsection{Data sources, selection, and witnesses}
The design plans 10,000 $9\times9$ puzzles: 2,500 from each of the easy,
medium, hard, and diabolical source labels in Sudoku Exchange at commit
\texttt{d8c8ebaee0c08c412cfba96af1923dfa61c83317}~\cite{sudoku-exchange-data}.
URLs, source files/lines, SHA-256, license/attribution, and original ratings
will be retained; ratings are source labels, not independently calibrated difficulty.
Deduplication uses canonical puzzle content across source groups, synthetic
families, pilot/main phases, and the old S80, rather than only hashes of
different serializations. Public duplicate ownership follows easy, medium,
hard, diabolical order and then ascending source line; excluded copies retain
both provenances in the log. After exclusions, fixed ranking selects the first
2,500 per group for main and the next two for pilot. Insufficient unique supply
fails the data gate; it does not authorize substitution or a reduced quota.

The planned master seed is 20261019. Deterministic ordering uses the contract's
SHA-256 rank over seed, operation label, and ID, sorted by digest and then ID;
it does not depend on Python's salted hash. Derived seeds, labels, and
resampling indices will be recorded before measurement, without speed-based
puzzle selection. Every selected puzzle must have a complete SAT witness
independently checked for domains, rows, columns, blocks, and clues before
freeze. Failure to find or validate a selected public witness blocks acceptance,
rather than silently replacing it with an easier puzzle. Witnesses remain
separate from solver inputs. Synthetic instances will carry
\texttt{uniqueness=unverified}; public instances may carry
\texttt{source\_claimed} only with a cited source. A valid witness does not
establish uniqueness.

\subsection{Synthetic roots and two transformation families}
The synthetic target is 5,000 $16\times16$ and 1,000 $25\times25$ puzzles.
Five target clue levels are 20\%, 30\%, 40\%, 50\%, and 60\%: 1,000 N16
and 200 N25 puzzles per level. N16 uses 1,000 roots and N25 uses 200 roots,
with five masks per root. Families are balanced: 500 roots/family for N16 and
100 roots/family for N25. A root is the final transformed completed grid,
not the common initial pattern.

The \texttt{permutation} family uses legal value, band/row, and stack/column
permutations. The \texttt{row\_cycle} family starts from a legally permuted
grid, selects two distinct rows in one band, and exchanges entries in all
columns belonging to one complete nontrivial cycle of their value mapping.
The cycle must have length $2,\ldots,N-1$; a whole-row exchange alone does not
qualify as the second family. If no proper cycle is available, the base/row
pair is resampled deterministically without changing family. The checker will
validate each transformed root and every witness. Row indices, cycle
values/columns, base hash, family, root ID, and resampling history are retained.

For each root and integer percentage $p$, retain
$\lfloor pN^2/100+0.5\rfloor$ cells by mask rank; actual clue count and actual
density $g/N^2$ are stored separately from the requested level. Masks are
ranked independently across densities, without a nesting guarantee. Roots
must be exactly distinct; a duplicate puzzle triggers mask resampling within
the same root/family/quota, while a duplicate root triggers regeneration of
that root slot before masking. After 10,000 failed attempts for a slot, stop
with evidence rather than substitute a family. Exact deduplication does not
establish uniform sampling or distinct isomorphism classes.

\subsection{Batches, configuration matrix, and separate pilot}
The plan fixes 100 main batches before measurement. Each contains 100 public
N9 puzzles (25/group), 50 synthetic N16 puzzles, and 10 synthetic N25 puzzles,
for 160 puzzles. Each batch receives five N16 roots per family and one N25
root per family, retaining all five masks of each root in that batch. Thus
N16 has ten puzzles/level (five/family), while N25 has two/level (one/family).
Batch and job order are frozen before execution, without selecting batches
by observed performance or estimated speed.

The eight planned N9 configurations are Glucose3 with pairwise, sequential
counter, and bitwise AMO; CP-SAT; HiGHS; Gurobi; CPLEX MIP; and CP Optimizer.
N16/N25 use the three SAT configurations, CP-SAT, and HiGHS. Excluding the
three commercial engines on large grids is a scope policy based on observed
limits, not proof that every large instance fails. The v2 matrix will retain
reasons, evidence/hashes, and thread settings; 18 included and six excluded
cells imply a planned 3,300 runs/batch. Excluded cells create neither timed
jobs nor fake TIMEOUT rows. No license purchase/application is planned.
Eligibility changes require an explicit pre-freeze decision and revised
denominators, not matrix reduction after observing failures.

The separate planned pilot contains 28 puzzles: eight public (two/group) and
ten each N16/N25 (one additional root/family at each size, all five masks).
Pilot roots are outside main root quotas; pilot inputs are disjoint from
main and old inputs. One repetition gives a planned $8\times8+20\times5=164$
runs, not a three-repetition main sample. SAT/UNSAT/invalid correctness cases
and artificial fixtures are also separate from pilot/main. Only accepted
pilot walltime will inform operational estimates; completion of all 330,000
runs before the deadline is not promised.

\subsection{Planned timing, storage, and recovery}
Main execution plans three repetitions with a 10-second cutoff for build
(including setup, solver creation/loading) plus solve; solve receives only
the remaining budget. Imports/input reading, decoding/checking/cleanup, and
checkpointing are outside build+solve. START/END synchronization and the
Windows Job Object hard guard are reused; the 60-second startup and
30-second postprocessing watchdogs are separate from the solve cutoff.
Every job has fresh model/solver state, one worker/thread, and sequential
execution without concurrent witness generation, builds, or heavy workloads.
CNF is not persisted for each job. Attempt wall surrounds \texttt{run\_job}
and includes startup/checking/cleanup; checkpoint time measures commit
separately, while session wall also includes scheduling/I/O/idle. Exact
component breakdowns are not inferred by subtraction when the API does not
expose them. Timings unknown after forced termination remain null, not zero.

The v2 design uses SQLite as the result authority: a RUNNING claim commits
before solving; result, SAT witness, checker evidence, and primary terminal
attempt commit atomically after solving. Checkpoint telemetry follows that
commit; absent telemetry cannot authorize rerunning a committed job. An
exclusive OS lock prevents concurrent writers; resume checks fingerprints
of code/data/config/matrix/schedule/checker/environment/runtime. A crash after
solving but before commit can lose an observation, so exactly-once solving
is not claimed. Explicit, reasoned resume may recover only PENDING or orphan
INTERRUPTED jobs after proving the old owner/worker is gone; all attempts
remain visible, and terminal SAT/TIMEOUT/ERROR jobs are not retried. Invalid
solutions, UNSAT on witnessed SAT, cleanup failures, or unexpected error
statuses retain their observation and halt rather than bypassing the failing
configuration.

The planned boundary for starting new jobs is 22:30 on 19 October 2026,
Asia/Saigon, reserving QA/report time before the 23:59 deadline. A running
job still finishes under its guards. Resume after this boundary needs a
reason and an audited future operational boundary; it neither changes the
scientific cutoff nor automatically permits later observations in the
pre-deadline report.

\subsection{Planned batch selection and statistical definitions}
Only main \texttt{qa\_complete} batches may be exported through a consistent
SQLite snapshot: the entire eligible matrix and all three repetitions must
be present, statuses limited to SAT/TIMEOUT, all SAT solutions independently
rechecked, identity/hash/timing/count/balance/root checks intact, and no
unresolved interrupted job or fatal flag. TIMEOUT does not make a batch
incomplete. ERROR and other excluded statuses cannot be dropped to shrink
denominators. All partial data remain available for later recovery;
pilot/artificial/partial observations are not final main evidence. Report
planned/started/execution-complete/QA-complete batches and exclusion reasons,
without claiming all 16,000 puzzles from a pre-deadline subset.

Runtime for an instance/configuration is the median of all three primary SAT
repetitions, separately for build/solve/total. Mixed SAT/TIMEOUT retains its
status vector and has no successful-only median. Group median, p90, and p95
use per-instance medians; quantiles use type 7 with $h=(m-1)q$ and linear
interpolation between floor/ceil order positions, for $q=0.90,0.95$.
For $m=0$ return null; for $m=1$ use the sole value; always report $m$ and
missing/mixed counts. Time components are aggregated independently: group
build and solve medians are not added to reconstruct the group total median.

PAR-2 for a primary attempt is observed total for SAT or 20 seconds for
TIMEOUT. ERROR/UNAVAILABLE/LICENSE\_LIMIT/INVALID\_INPUT/UNSAT/INTERRUPTED
have no penalty, not a timeout or zero. Per-instance PAR-2 is the mean of
three penalties only when all three attempts are eligible; the stratum mean
over instances equals the attempt mean for complete three-repetition blocks.
Publish both attempt and instance denominators. Coverage distinguishes target,
matrix-planned, selected complete blocks/jobs, observed primary, solved
primary, any-SAT, all-three-SAT, timeouts, and every excluded status; historical
interrupted attempts are not additional independent samples.

Paired comparisons use identical IDs in the same eligible batches/strata
with three SAT repetitions on both sides; report IDs/counts and the median
of per-instance A/B runtime ratios. A zero denominator yields null, not
infinity; division of group medians is not a substitute. Stratify by N,
source/rating group, clue level, and family, retaining original ratings and
actual clues. Do not pool timing averages from the old 906-run and expanded
experiments.

\subsection{Limitations and inputs required before results}
Three repetitions are not three independent puzzles. Five masks sharing a
root form a cluster; if uncertainty intervals are computed, resample roots
jointly with all five masks within N/family while retaining density strata;
public resampling uses instances. State the method, seed, and counts or omit
intervals when unsupported. Both transformation families remain biased;
uniformity, non-isomorphism, and uniqueness are not established. Fewer clues
are not treated as proof of greater difficulty, and public N9 versus synthetic
N16/N25 is not a size-only complexity experiment. Engine scope, one machine,
the cutoff, and inclusion of only QA-complete batches limit generalization;
coverage and partial observations must remain explicit.

Before results integration, SAT08 must supply QA-approved export/summary,
selected/excluded batch and ID sets, denominators, coverage/mixed-status/
runtime/PAR-2/paired tables, quantile/cluster definitions, figures/captions,
and the SQLite--CSV--summary--tables/figures hash chain. Frozen dataset,
matrix, execution, and environment metadata plus the pilot estimate must
accompany the handoff from their respective owners. Only after these gates
may expanded Results/Abstract/Conclusion and both PDFs be updated in a later
authorized package.
